My interest in product work comes from a fairly simple question: what makes a piece of technology useful to the person using it?

At Ohio State, I’m studying Management Information Systems at the Fisher College of Business. I’m interested in how research and data can inform decisions, and in the practical work of turning those decisions into something people can use.

What I’ve learned through my work

At PNC, I spent time with digital banking session replays, funnels, and customer feedback. That work taught me to look beyond a metric and ask what the experience felt like to the person behind it.

At Imageomics, I’ve approached the question through building. I helped migrate and redesign the NSF HDR research platform, implementing navigation, search, and shared page structures with direction from a designer. Working with research stakeholders made the content and its accuracy as important as the code.

My time at ODOT added another perspective: a small reporting improvement can be useful without introducing a new system. In a setting shaped by quality standards, the details matter.

How I approach problems

I start by listening. I write things down, ask what the evidence actually supports, and make tradeoffs clear. I care about leaving work understandable for the next person who picks it up.

That mix of customer understanding, analysis, and implementation is what draws me toward product management and digital strategy.

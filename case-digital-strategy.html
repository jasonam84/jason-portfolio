Digital strategy & analytics

College of Education and Human Ecology · Ohio State · 2024–2025

This work sat between analytics, communication, and day-to-day decision-making. The question was rarely just "what happened in the data?" It was usually "what should we actually do with this, given the team and time we have?"

I handled reporting, interpretation, and recommendation support for non-technical stakeholders. The goal was not to produce more dashboards. It was to help teams focus on patterns that mattered and ignore noise.

Context

Teams needed a clearer view of how their digital content was performing, but they did not need another pile of metrics with no next step attached. Bandwidth was limited, priorities competed, and not every insight was worth acting on.

My role

  • Reviewed traffic and engagement patterns across digital content
  • Helped build reporting cadences that stakeholders could actually keep up with
  • Translated analytics into plain-language takeaways and recommendations
  • Framed tradeoffs around effort, timing, and likely impact

What made it tricky

  • Non-technical audiences needed clarity, not analytics jargon
  • Teams had limited time to act on recommendations
  • Not every metric represented something worth changing
  • Recommendations had to fit real constraints, not ideal ones

How I approached it

I tried to keep the work practical. If a metric was interesting but not decision-useful, I said so. If a recommendation was technically sound but unrealistic for the team’s bandwidth, I adjusted it.

  • Focused on directional patterns instead of overexplaining weekly swings
  • Tied analytics back to content goals and team priorities
  • Recommended smaller, more realistic actions over sweeping changes
  • Kept reporting understandable enough that people would keep using it

Impact

  • Made reporting easier for non-technical teams to understand and use
  • Helped stakeholders separate signal from noise when reviewing performance
  • Improved the quality of recommendation conversations by grounding them in actual constraints
  • Supported more practical decisions about what content to improve, revisit, or leave alone

What this work reinforced

Analytics work is only useful when it changes what a team does next. Good reporting is not about surfacing more numbers. It is about making the next step clearer.
